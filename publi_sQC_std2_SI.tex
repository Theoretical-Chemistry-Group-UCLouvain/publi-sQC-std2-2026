\section{Validity of the CNDO approximation}

Within the CNDO approximation, the two-center quantities entering the simplified ERIs are written as
\begin{equation}
	(\lambda_\mu\lambda_\mu|\lambda_\nu\lambda_\nu)
	= \sum_{\alpha\beta\kappa\eta} (\alpha\beta|\kappa\eta)\,
	S^{-1/2}_{\mu\alpha}S^{-1/2}_{\mu\beta}S^{-1/2}_{\nu\kappa}S^{-1/2}_{\nu\eta}.
	\label{eq:loweri}
\end{equation}
To analyze this expression, we expand $\mathbf S^{-1/2}$ as a power series,
$\mathbf S^{-1/2} = (\mathbf I + \bm\Delta)^{-1/2}
= \mathbf I - \tfrac{1}{2}\bm\Delta + \frac{3}{8}\bm\Delta^2 + \mathcal{O}(\bm\Delta^3)$,
with $S_{\mu\nu} = \delta_{\mu\nu} + \Delta_{\mu\nu}$.  
Substituting this expansion into Eq.~\eqref{eq:loweri} yields
\begin{align}
	(\lambda_\mu\lambda_\mu|\lambda_\nu\lambda_\nu)  = (\mu\mu|\nu\nu) &-\sum_{\alpha\neq\mu} \Delta_{\mu\alpha} (\alpha\mu|\nu\nu) -\sum_{\alpha\neq\nu} \Delta_{\nu\alpha} (\mu\mu|\alpha\nu)\nonumber\\
	&+\frac{3}{4}\sum_{\alpha\beta\neq \mu} \Delta_{\mu\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha\beta} (\beta\mu|\nu\nu)+\frac{3}{4}\sum_{\alpha\beta\neq \nu} \Delta_{\nu\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha\beta} (\mu\mu|\beta\nu) \nonumber\\
	&+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\alpha\beta\neq \mu} \Delta_{\mu\alpha}\Delta_{\mu\beta} (\alpha\beta|\nu\nu)+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\alpha\beta\neq \nu} \Delta_{\nu\alpha}\Delta_{\nu\beta} (\mu\mu|\alpha\beta) \nonumber\\
	&+\sum_{\alpha\neq\mu,\beta\neq\nu} \Delta_{\mu\alpha}\Delta_{\nu\beta} (\alpha\mu|\beta\nu) + \mathcal{O}(\bm\Delta^3).
	\label{eq:cndo-approx}
\end{align}
which provides a systematic hierarchy of approximations.  
In practice, Eq.~\eqref{eq:cndo-approx} can be truncated at different orders, leading to the 0\textsuperscript{th}-order approximation 
$[(\lambda_\mu\lambda_\mu|\lambda_\nu\lambda_\nu) \approx (\mu\mu|\nu\nu)]$, 
the first-order approximation (including the linear terms in $\Delta$), 
or the second-order approximation (including the terms in $\Delta^2$).

The impact of this truncation on the ERIs is illustrated in Figs.~\ref{fig:cndo-lih}--\ref{fig:cndo-c2h4}, which report the mean absolute error (MAE) on the $(pq|rs)$ integrals for representative bond deformations in small molecules.  
Note that all the (atomic) two-electron integrals are evaluated exactly.
For simple systems such as \ce{LiH} (Fig.~\ref{fig:cndo-lih}), short bond lengths lead to large AO overlaps ($\Delta_{\alpha\beta} \gg 0$), which accentuate the poor convergence behavior of the $\Delta$ power series in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cndo-approx} (especially for large basis sets and number of atoms, see cc-pvTZ results).\cite{chandlerReexaminationJustificationNeglect1980}
In contrast, at larger bond distances the overlaps become small and the truncated expansion provides a more reliable approximation.  
Interestingly, the 0\textsuperscript{th}-order approximation consistently follows the ``exact'' limit rather closely for all systems over the full range of bond distances considered.
Overall, near equilibrium geometries the MAE on the ERIs in the MO basis is on the order of \SI{e-2}{\hartree}, and decreases with increasing basis set size, reaching values below \SI{e-3}{\hartree}.

\section{OR45 benchmark}

Previous TD results were taken from Ref.~\citenum{dewergifosseSimplifiedTimedependentDensity2020}, and the corresponding wavefunctions were used as input for the subsequent (X)sPBE0, (X)sB3LYP, and (X)sBHHLYP calculations.  
Additional TD calculations, with hybrid and range-separated hybrid functionals (M06-2X, CAM-B3LYP, and $\omega$B97X-D3), were performed with \textsc{Gaussian~16} using the aug-cc-pVDZ basis set in conjunction with gauge-including atomic orbitals (GIAOs).  
The molecular geometries (Fig.~\ref{fig:or-compounds}) were also taken from Ref.~\citenum{dewergifosseSimplifiedTimedependentDensity2020} and used without further optimization.  
All resulting wavefunctions were subsequently employed as input for the corresponding (X)sTD calculations.
Following Ref.~\citenum{dewergifosseSimplifiedTimedependentDensity2020}, (X)sTD calculations were performed using $E_{thr}=\SI{50}{\electronvolt}$ (reduced to \SI{20}{\electronvolt} for compounds \textbf{36}-\textbf{42}).

The performance of the different methods is summarized in Tables~\ref{tab:or45-old} (previous XCFs) and \ref{tab:or45-new} (new XCFs), which report mean and median deviations of the calculated $[\Phi]_D$ from experiment.  
Because the dataset spans several orders of magnitude, both global statistics and subset-specific results are provided for groups of compounds with similar responses, following Ref.~\citenum{dewergifosseSimplifiedTimedependentDensity2020}.  
Unsigned absolute and relative mean ($\Delta$) and median ($\delta$) deviations are reported; significant differences between $\Delta$ and $\delta$ typically indicate the presence of outliers.  
As noted in the original OR45 study,\cite{srebroOpticalRotationCalculated2011} relative deviations can become very large for molecules with small optical rotations (compounds \textbf{1}--\textbf{13}), including cases where the predicted sign is incorrect.  
These systems are dominated by excited states with significant Rydberg character, for which both TD and simplified TD approaches perform poorly ($\Delta^r > \SI{100}{\percent}$), although XsTD generally yields smaller $\delta^r$ values than sTD.  
In contrast, compounds with predominant $\pi \to \pi^\star$ character (in \textbf{14}--\textbf{42}) are more reliably described.  

While B3LYP and PBE0 remain robust overall choices (yielding the smallest $\Delta^r$ and $\delta^r$), certain subsets (e.g., six-membered rings, \textbf{19}--\textbf{30}, and helicenes, \textbf{38}--\textbf{42}) benefit from RSH functionals, with, for instance, $\Delta^r < \SI{20}{\percent}$ and $\delta^r < \SI{10}{\percent}$ for helicenes.  
In this context, sCAM-B3LYP and XsCAM-B3LYP emerge as competitive alternatives, although the former generally performs more consistently (with performances similar to CAM-B3LYP) across all compounds studied here (\textbf{1}--\textbf{42}).  
Interestingly, XsPBE0, XsBHHLYP, and XsM06-2X can outperform their sTD counterparts for helicenes, highlighting a non-uniform behavior across functionals that warrants further investigation.  
Finally, the MVG formulation does not provide significant improvements over LG (except in a few specific cases), likely because the molecules were positioned close to their center of charge.
